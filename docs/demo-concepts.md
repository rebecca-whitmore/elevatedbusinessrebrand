# Brand styles for six aesthetics website concepts

Creative direction for Rebecca and Codex

We are developing six distinct website concepts for solo aesthetics practitioners in the UK. Each should help a prospective client recognise the business she wants to become and imagine feeling proud of her own website. Together, the concepts should demonstrate Rebecca’s range as a designer while sharing a consistently high standard of execution.

This document defines the personality, visual language and overall impression of each concept. Use it to guide design decisions as the demos develop. Page structures, section sequences, final fonts and exact colour values will be agreed separately.

### How to use this direction

Treat each concept as a coherent identity. Its colour, typography, imagery and visual rhythm should express the same personality. Tailoring a demo to a practitioner should preserve that identity while making room for her name, voice, treatments and photography. A change of palette alone should never account for the difference between two concepts.

### A shared standard

Every concept should feel premium, credible and welcoming. Visitors should understand who the practitioner is, what she offers and how to enquire. Readable typography and clear actions belong in all six styles. The aesthetic should suit a personal practice without relying on the scale, premises or reputation of a large clinic.

### The six personalities

Quiet luxury expresses quiet elegance. Modern editorial brings fashion sensibility and compositional confidence. Soft feminine luxury centres warmth and personal confidence. Organic and natural offers an earthy, tactile calm. Clinical contemporary communicates precision and expertise. Bold boutique creates a richer, more expressive brand presence.

## AMBER - Quiet luxury

*Quiet elegance with an assured personal presence*

### Personality and impression

Composed, discreet and carefully considered. The website should feel like entering a beautifully kept private practice where someone has paid attention to every detail. Its appeal comes from restraint and proportion. A practitioner choosing this concept wants her business to feel refined and established, with a welcome that remains personal.

### Colour and material character

Build around cream, warm ivory, stone and taupe, anchored by deep brown or charcoal for readable contrast. Tonal differences can be subtle, provided the hierarchy stays clear. Surfaces should feel smooth and finely finished. Any texture should be almost imperceptible; overtly rustic materials would pull the identity towards organic and natural.

### Typography and visual rhythm

Use an elegant, restrained serif with a clear supporting sans serif. Give typography room to breathe and use scale with discipline. Generous space, balanced proportions and a small number of deliberate details should establish the mood. The site should feel fully designed even in areas with very little imagery.

### Imagery and human presence

Choose fewer, stronger photographs with soft light, composed framing and a consistent neutral colour treatment. A thoughtful practitioner portrait, skin detail or a genuine detail of the practice can carry considerable weight. People belong here, but the photography should have a quieter emotional register than soft feminine luxury.

### Voice and visitor relationship

Write with calm assurance and specific, considered language. Personal attention should feel evident without exaggerated claims of exclusivity. The visitor should sense that she has time to consider her options and can approach the practitioner comfortably.

### What keeps it distinctive

Preserve neutral warmth, visual restraint and a sense of quiet elegance. Blush-led romance, busy image sequences and conspicuous gold decoration would change its character. If it feels empty or anonymous, add a meaningful human detail or improve the typography before adding ornament.

## BELLA - Modern editorial

*Fashion sensibility expressed through strong composition*

### Personality and impression

Visually assured, contemporary and selective. The website should have the presence of a well-directed beauty editorial, with a practitioner whose taste feels current and considered. It appeals to someone who wants a sophisticated, design-conscious identity and is comfortable with a more expressive visual presentation.

### Colour and material character

Use black, white and controlled greys as the foundation. A single restrained accent may sharpen the identity, but composition should supply most of the impact. Crisp contrasts and clear changes of scale matter more than decorative finishes. The black-and-white foundation should remain recognisable across the experience.

### Typography and visual rhythm

Let typography actively shape the design. Oversized headlines, expressive serif forms or a distinctive display face can sit alongside disciplined supporting text. Use deliberate cropping, variation in scale and occasional asymmetry to create tension. These choices should read as authored and purposeful while preserving a clear reading order.

### Imagery and human presence

Choose fashion-inspired beauty portraits, expressive crops and striking light or monochrome treatments. Each photograph should feel selected for a visual purpose. Keep skin and expression believable. The practitioner’s presence should retain warmth so that the site feels suitable for a personal appointment as well as visually compelling.

### Voice and visitor relationship

Use concise, precise copy with confident headlines. Let clear supporting information explain the treatments and practitioner. The visual language can be unconventional; navigation and enquiry wording should remain familiar enough for visitors to act without decoding the design.

### What keeps it distinctive

Its signature is graphic composition and the relationship between type and photography. Preserve those qualities even when images or business names change. Deep burgundy, sumptuous finishes and overt glamour belong more naturally to bold boutique. Avoid turning editorial expression into giant text everywhere or awkward reading on mobile.

## CLARA - Soft feminine luxury

*Warm femininity with a strong sense of personal confidence*

### Personality and impression

Gentle, polished and emotionally welcoming. The website should help a visitor picture feeling comfortable, cared for and confident in herself. A practitioner choosing this concept wants her business to feel beautiful and aspirational while remaining approachable. Femininity should feel assured and adult.

### Colour and material character

Make blush and cream the defining combination, supported by ivory and a restrained dusty rose. Use a deeper warm brown or muted plum where text and actions need contrast. Keep pink visibly present so this identity has its own character beside quiet luxury. Avoid allowing pale tones to make the whole page indistinct.

### Typography and visual rhythm

Use graceful serif typography with lightness in its forms and a highly readable supporting face. Softer contours and a gentle rhythm can give the design warmth. Space should feel generous but lived in, with photography carrying more of the experience than in quiet luxury. Delicacy should come from proportion rather than tiny text.

### Imagery and human presence

Give people a larger role: warmly lit portraits of women, natural expressions and a sense of ease in their own skin. This can support more photography than quiet luxury, provided each image adds something. Include different adult ages and believable skin texture. Select images for warmth and self-possession rather than a repeated model pose. Stock or demo photography should not be presented as proof of treatment results.

### Voice and visitor relationship

Use warm, respectful language that makes personal care feel individual. The visitor should sense an approachable relationship with the practitioner. Avoid language that implies she needs to correct herself to deserve confidence, or that assumes every woman shares the same beauty goal.

### What keeps it distinctive

Preserve the blush-and-cream identity, human warmth and a more visible emotional connection through photography. Quiet luxury is calmer and more reserved. Avoid girlish styling, decorative scripts, glitter effects or a page full of interchangeable smiling portraits. The character should remain sophisticated even when the palette is soft.

## DAPHNE - Organic and natural

*Earthy calm with a tactile sense of care*

### Personality and impression

Grounded, thoughtful and quietly restorative. The website should suggest an unhurried environment where skin and the individual receive careful attention. It suits a practitioner drawn to a warmer, more tactile identity, with a strong interest in skin care and a personal approach. The overall impression should remain relevant to an aesthetics practice.

### Colour and material character

Use sage and olive as recognisable colours, balanced with oat, warm off-white and earthy brown. A restrained clay accent can add warmth. Introduce the feeling of linen, paper, stone or softly lit natural surfaces through subtle texture and photography. Green and material character should be substantial enough to distinguish this from another cream luxury design.

### Typography and visual rhythm

Choose a serif with warmth and character or a gently humanist typeface, supported by clear everyday text. Allow a relaxed pace and occasional softer shapes. Keep alignment and hierarchy deliberate so that the organic quality feels composed. Avoid scattering abstract blobs, leaves and decorative curves across an otherwise conventional page.

### Imagery and human presence

Favour daylight, natural skin texture, tactile close-ups and warm portraits. Botanical or material details can set the atmosphere, but people and the real practice should remain recognisable. The original reference uses an apothecary mood; translate its material warmth into the practitioner’s world rather than making the website feel like a skincare shop.

### Voice and visitor relationship

Use measured, grounded language with space to explain a considered approach. The visitor should feel that she will be listened to. The natural aesthetic describes the brand mood; it should not imply that injectables are plant-based, risk-free or inherently natural. Any statements about treatments must come from the practitioner’s actual offer.

### What keeps it distinctive

Protect the earthy palette, tactile surfaces and relaxed human presence. Quiet luxury should feel smoother and more reserved; this concept should feel closer to natural materials and everyday skin. Avoid drifting into rustic craft, generic spa imagery or an identity dominated by product bottles and foliage.

## ELSIE - Clinical contemporary

*Modern precision with an approachable practitioner*

### Personality and impression

Clear, capable and reassuring. The website should give visitors a sense of order and confidence in the person providing treatment. It suits a practitioner who wants expertise and considered decision-making to be central to her identity. Personal warmth should remain visible within that precise presentation.

### Colour and material character

Use crisp white as the dominant foundation, supported by cool stone, pale grey and a restrained blue-grey or similar muted accent. Anchor the palette with charcoal or deep navy. Clean finishes and clear visual boundaries should create the impression of precision. Strong readability matters more than maintaining a uniformly pale appearance.

### Typography and visual rhythm

Use a contemporary sans serif with excellent legibility, clear weights and disciplined spacing. A restrained secondary face is possible, but hierarchy should feel systematic. Compositions should be orderly and economical, with enough space to make information easy to absorb. This concept can feel premium through clarity and consistency.

### Imagery and human presence

Prioritise the real practitioner, a well-presented consultation environment and clear, naturally lit portraits. Relevant practice details can add credibility without relying on equipment imagery. The original thumbnail’s calm treatment room supports the mood, but the wider identity should give the visitor a person to connect with. Avoid decorative scientific diagrams that imply evidence or expertise the business has not established.

### Voice and visitor relationship

Use plain, specific explanations and a respectful tone. Give the visitor a sense that questions are welcome and choices will be explained. Qualifications and professional details should be accurate to the individual. The visual style alone should never imply a medical title or credential.

### What keeps it distinctive

Preserve crispness, orderly typography and an emphasis on understanding the practitioner’s approach. Quiet luxury also uses space, but its character comes from warm refinement; this concept should communicate precision more directly. Avoid generic corporate blue, cold hospital imagery or reducing the personal practice to a catalogue of treatments.

## FRANCESCA - Bold boutique

*Rich colour and a memorable personal identity*

### Personality and impression

Confident, expressive and intimate. The website should feel like a distinctive independent beauty destination with a recognisable point of view. It appeals to a practitioner who wants her business to be remembered and is comfortable with a stronger brand presence. The welcome should feel personal enough to support a solo practice.

### Colour and material character

Build around burgundy and chocolate, using warm cream and a restrained rose or copper note to provide light and contrast. Let rich colour occupy meaningful space rather than appearing only on buttons. Satin-like light, depth and carefully chosen tonal contrasts can create a sumptuous feel without depending on metallic decoration.

### Typography and visual rhythm

Choose a distinctive display face or a characterful serif that can become part of the brand’s identity. Balance it with simple, readable supporting typography. Use confident proportions and a deliberate alternation between richer and quieter moments. Strong branding should be visible across the experience, including smaller details.

### Imagery and human presence

Choose expressive beauty portraits, richer lighting and close crops with a cohesive colour treatment. Photography can be glamorous while retaining believable faces and skin. The practitioner should have a clear presence so the identity feels owned by someone. Translate the source reference’s burgundy and dramatic imagery into warmth and confidence suitable for an appointment-based business.

### Voice and visitor relationship

Use assured, personable language with a recognisable voice. The visitor should remember the practice and feel welcome to approach it. Keep treatment explanations direct and avoid making every sentence sound like an advertising headline. Confidence should come from a clear identity and offer.

### What keeps it distinctive

Its signature is saturated warmth and expressive branding. Modern editorial relies more on graphic tension, monochrome and the composition of type and image. Keep that distinction visible. Avoid nightclub darkness, aggressive sales language, excessive gloss or rich backgrounds that make reading tiring. Quiet passages should give the stronger moments room to work.
