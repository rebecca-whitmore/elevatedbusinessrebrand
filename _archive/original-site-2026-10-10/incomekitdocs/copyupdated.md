# Aesthetics Income Engine Sales Page

## Revised copy direction for sense checking

**Status:** Proposed messaging and page structure only. This is not the final sales copy.

## Overall direction

The revised page should feel confident, commercially aware and easy to act on. It should make the reader recognise that there may be unrealised booking opportunities inside the client list they already have, then position Aesthetics Income Engine as the practical way to start those conversations.

The sales argument is not that email guarantees bookings. It is that relying only on new social posts, offers and discounts can leave an existing business asset unused. The buyer already has people who know the practice. The kit removes the uncertainty around what to send, who to send it to and what to do next.

The page should make £27 feel like a straightforward, low-risk decision by showing the product clearly, building the value of every component and giving the reader a genuine reason to act before the launch price changes.

## Messaging spine

- **Reader:** UK medically qualified solo aesthetics practitioners with past clients they may be able to contact appropriately by email.
- **What they want:** More consistent booking conversations and a practical way to encourage suitable past clients to reconnect.
- **Current frustration:** Quiet periods can lead to more posting, another promotion or discounting, while previous clients remain untouched in the booking system.
- **The offer:** Ready-written reactivation and follow-up emails, decision guidance, implementation instructions, ongoing email frameworks, sales support and a tracker.
- **The mechanism:** Select appropriate past clients, adapt a suitable email, send it through appropriate software, manage responses and follow up.
- **The purchase case:** The strategic thinking and wording have already been prepared. The buyer can move from good intention to a real campaign without building everything from scratch.
- **Primary action:** Purchase Aesthetics Income Engine for the confirmed launch price.

## Voice

The tone should be:

- Direct and assured
- Commercial without sounding aggressive
- Warm and practical
- Respectful of professional responsibilities
- More conversational than instructional
- Clear about limitations without repeatedly interrupting the sale

The copy can use short, decisive lines such as “Your client list should not be sitting quietly in your booking system” and “Stop wondering what to send”. These should be balanced by precise explanations of what the kit does and does not provide.

## Recommended page journey

### 0. Launch announcement

**Purpose:** Introduce genuine urgency before the reader reaches the offer.

State the £27 launch price, the future £49 price and the confirmed deadline. A countdown can reinforce this once the exact date and time are decided.

Keep this short. It should create momentum, not delay the opening message.

### 1. Opening offer

**Purpose:** Make the audience, opportunity, product and price immediately clear.

Lead with a stronger commercial idea, along the lines of:

> Your next booking conversation may already be sitting in your client list.

Introduce Aesthetics Income Engine as the ready-to-use email marketing kit that helps eligible UK aesthetics practitioners reconnect with past clients and keep useful contact going.

Emphasise that the emails, follow-ups and decision process are already prepared. The buyer chooses suitable clients, personalises the wording and sends.

Show the £27 price prominently, list the core kit and three bonuses in one compact value statement, then use a direct purchase button.

The guarantee can be mentioned briefly here, but the detailed conditions should appear later.

### 2. The problem and cost of inaction

**Purpose:** Make the reader care about solving the problem now.

Describe the familiar cycle of a quiet diary leading to more posting, last-minute offers or reduced prices. Contrast that activity with the past-client list already sitting inside the business.

The emotional emphasis should not be fear. It should be frustration at overlooking an existing opportunity and relief that there is a more organised action to take.

Key ideas:

- The practice has already done the work of attracting and serving these clients.
- Some past clients may simply need a relevant reason and an easy way to reconnect.
- Waiting for them to remember the practice leaves the next step to chance.
- Continuing to wonder what to write does not create conversations.

End by introducing the kit as the bridge between “I should email my clients” and actually sending a considered campaign.

### 3. Introduce the solution

**Purpose:** Explain why this product is different from receiving a folder of generic templates.

Present Aesthetics Income Engine as a complete reactivation process, not merely five emails.

It includes the wording, recipient-selection guidance, follow-up decisions, response scenarios and an ongoing plan. The reader should understand that they are buying clarity around the whole task.

This is also the place to reinforce that no discount or complicated loyalty scheme is required. The starting point is a useful, professional invitation to reconnect.

### 4. Show what they will use

**Purpose:** Replace missing testimonials with tangible product evidence.

Show a polished preview of one email and enough of the template to demonstrate the tone and usability. Pair it with the Amber, Bella and Claire worked scenarios.

Frame those scenarios as proof that the kit answers the questions that appear after sending:

- What if nobody replies immediately?
- What if someone asks a question?
- What if someone books?
- When should the automated sequence stop?

Follow the preview with the core inclusions, written as benefits rather than a plain contents list.

Examples of the emphasis:

- Three opening emails so the message can match the length of the client’s absence.
- Two polite follow-ups so the buyer does not have to guess whether or when to contact them again.
- A client-selection guide to support more considered recipient choices.
- Worked response scenarios so replies and bookings lead to the right next action.

Add a purchase prompt after this section because the reader has now seen what they are buying.

### 5. Three steps to send the first campaign

**Purpose:** Make implementation feel manageable.

Keep the existing three-step process:

1. Choose appropriate clients.
2. Adapt and send the selected email.
3. Follow up, manage responses and begin an ongoing contact routine.

The copy should make it clear that the buyer does not need to replace suitable software they already use. Mention the selected MailerLite tutorial guide as implementation support, not as a requirement.

The main feeling here should be: “I can see exactly how I would do this.”

### 6. Build the value of the complete package

**Purpose:** Make the offer feel significantly more valuable than the purchase price.

Present the core kit first, then transition into the bonuses as the tools that help turn one campaign into an ongoing business habit.

The core value is immediate action. The bonus value is continuity.

#### Bonus 1: Keep-in-Touch Email Pack

Position this as the answer to “What do I send after the reactivation campaign?” It gives the buyer reasons to remain usefully visible throughout the year.

#### Bonus 2: From Reply to Appointment

Position this as support for converting interest into a clear and professional next step. Avoid suggesting that every reply becomes a booking.

#### Bonus 3: Email Planner & Results Tracker

Position this as the system that prevents follow-ups, replies and future sends from becoming scattered or forgotten.

Each bonus should end with a concise practical result. Together they should make the product feel like a small client-retention system rather than a one-off template pack.

### 7. Why Rebecca created it

**Purpose:** Add trust and a human reason to buy.

Introduce Rebecca briefly, using her established background in sales, customer service and relationship-led business support. Explain that the product was created to help practitioners turn an overlooked client list into a practical, repeatable contact routine.

The section should be personal but short. It should not imply clinical or regulatory expertise that has not been established. Rebecca’s authority here is in customer relationships, communication, sales thinking and making implementation easier.

Possible emphasis:

> I did not want to give you another collection of prompts and leave you to work out the difficult parts. I wanted you to know what to send, who it may suit and what to do when someone responds.

### 8. Price, urgency, guarantee and help

**Purpose:** Bring the accumulated value into one clear purchase decision.

Recap the entire package visually and state the launch price beside the future price. The copy should confidently explain that £27 buys the campaign wording, implementation process, ongoing email frameworks, conversation training and planning tools.

Introduce the real deadline and countdown once confirmed.

The guarantee should be framed positively as reassurance for buyers who implement the system. Keep the summary simple, then provide the complete qualification requirements clearly underneath or through a full terms link.

Do not allow the guarantee explanation to become the dominant sales message. It supports the decision but is not the main reason to buy.

Describe the help route only after its scope, eligibility and response time are confirmed.

Finish with a strong purchase button and clear delivery information.

### 9. FAQs

**Purpose:** Resolve final objections without repeating the entire page.

Retain questions about:

- Who the kit is for
- Whether an existing email list is required
- Recipient permission and eligibility
- Software requirements
- The work required from the buyer
- Whether results are guaranteed
- Discounts
- Product delivery
- Implementation help
- Guarantee conditions

Answers should remain clear and accurate, but they can be more encouraging. Lead with the direct answer, then explain any condition.

### 10. Final purchase invitation

**Purpose:** End on action and opportunity rather than caution.

Return to the central message: the reader already has people who know the practice, and this kit gives those suitable clients a clear way to reconnect.

Use short, active copy:

- Choose the right clients.
- Personalise the prepared email.
- Send the invitation.
- Follow the plan.

Restate the product name, everything included, the £27 launch price, the confirmed deadline and the purchase button.

Keep the guarantee qualification as a short supporting note with a link to the full requirements.

## CTA direction

Use clear purchase-focused buttons throughout. Recommended working label:

**Get Aesthetics Income Engine for £27**

This is more direct and natural than changing the CTA wording in every section. Supporting microcopy can clarify delivery and the future price once confirmed.

## Persuasion principles to retain in the final copy

- Sell the overlooked opportunity before explaining every feature.
- Make the cost of continued inaction visible without using fear or shame.
- Demonstrate the product early.
- Translate every inclusion into a practical reason it matters.
- Use the full package and bonuses to create value contrast with the £27 price.
- Repeat the purchase opportunity after major decision points.
- Keep compliance information accessible, but do not let caution flatten every claim.
- Use only genuine urgency tied to the confirmed price-rise deadline.
- Never imply guaranteed bookings or income.
- Clearly distinguish fictional worked examples from customer results.

3. Confirm the product delivery method and access timing.
4. Define the qualifying response covered by the guarantee.
5. Confirm the minimum recipient number, required follow-ups, claim deadline and acceptable evidence.
6. Confirm whether implementation help is included, what it covers and the expected response time.
7. Confirm the seller name, contact route and purchase platform.
8. Confirm whether Rebecca’s sales and customer-service background is the preferred trust angle for this product.
